*Weeks 6 and 7: Onboarding, test set, and demo (Milestone 3)*

*What your team does:*

1. Design the three-stage onboarding for your chatbot.
2. Fill the Feature Choice Board.
3. Write the accessibility plan.
4. Build the full test set.
5. Record a demo video of your chatbot.
6. Put the revised design pack together and keep your prompt log.

*What to present at the check-in*

*1. Onboarding storyboard.* Show the screens or messages for each of the three stages:
- The system does it: the chatbot shows the user an example of what it can do.
- Co-pilot: the user tries a task with guidance from the chatbot.
- The user does it: the user works alone, and help stays available on request.

Show how each of your three personas moves through the stages, and where your principle feature first appears.

*2. Feature Choice Board (6 to 9 features).*
For each feature, write:
- what it does
- which persona it serves
- which level of your HAI Principle Ladder it supports
- whether it is core or optional for the user

*3. Accessibility plan.*
It must cover:
- your documents (headings, readable fonts, alt text for diagrams, contrast)
- your chat interface (keyboard use, screen reader labels, text size, status shown in words and not only in colour)
- the accessibility need of your persona, and how the design meets it
- how you will test it

*4. Full test set (30 or more labeled prompts).*
Include prompts that test your principle feature, and prompts written from each persona's point of view.

*5. Final design pack (Milestone 3).*
One organized file with the revised personas, journey maps, onboarding storyboard, Choice Board, and accessibility plan.

*6. Demo video (3 to 5 minutes).*
A screen recording of your own working chatbot, with a voice-over or captions. It must show, in this order:
- Introduction (about 20 seconds): team name, project name, target users, and your HAI principle.
- Greeting: the chatbot's first message, including what it can and cannot do.
- One main task from start to finish: a real user request and the chatbot's real answer from your backend.
- Your principle feature in action: for example the "Why this?" explanation, the source link, the safety refusal, or the accept/edit/undo control. Say on the video which principle it shows.
- One problem case: an unclear, out-of-scope, or unsafe request, and how the chatbot recovers (clarifying question, fallback message, or human handover).
- Closing (about 20 seconds): what works now, and what is still planned.

Rules for the video:
- Record your own system running. _Slides, mock-ups, or a public AI website do not count._
- Use only fictional or synthetic data. Do not show API keys or personal information.
- Make the text on screen large enough to read.
