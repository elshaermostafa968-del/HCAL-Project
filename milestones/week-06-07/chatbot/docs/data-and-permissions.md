# Data provenance, permission, and bias review

All 40 student snippets, names, publication mentions, hints, and validation fixtures were created as fictional teaching examples for this project. No real student drafts, screenshots, personal records, or API keys are included.

The source guides remain the property of their publishers. Publicly readable does not mean openly licensed. This package links to the guides and uses short original summaries of rules, not copies of the manuals or an assertion that their content has a permissive license. The team should verify that its use meets course and institutional requirements before redistribution. A field named verified_reference_feedback is required by the brief; it does not certify expert review. Review the linked sources independently before graded submission.

## Proposed permission statement, pending team adoption

For now, use this as the requested course prototype and record the team's explicit license decision before public redistribution.

## Bias check

- No culture, language background, gender, name, or accessibility need determines a writing score or technical ability.
- Fictional samples use ordinary study/project contexts without ranking cultures or linking names to errors.
- Passive voice is not automatically wrong. Structural preferences are suggestions, not universal rules.
- Local instructions can override a general style convention for a particular assignment; conflicts go to the instructor.
- English-first retrieval is a limitation. Simple English labels and incremental hints help novices, but do not replace multilingual testing.
- Accessible labels are text, not color alone. Novice and advanced shortcuts coexist.
